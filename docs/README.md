# Undead of Night: Last Round

A Hall of the Gods, Inc. game. A top-down pixel art zombie survival game set in an open town at night. Play solo in the browser, or co-op over a small WebSocket server. Ammo and health are finite: you find them in the town's buildings, and every gunshot tells nearby zombies where you are.

## How it plays

- **Two styles**: Campaign and Arcade, chosen for each run on the Play page (the type is `GameStyle` in `shared/game-style.ts`; "mode" already means solo or multiplayer). *Arcade* is the game as it always was: ten levels in a row, the workbench in the van, the Levels page, Continue from level N. *Campaign* is the Home Loop: you live in an underground bunker and go out on trips. A trip is one fresh town; towns hold jewels, not coin (`TOWN_JEWELS` in `shared/treasure.ts`), and what is picked up is carried and only banked when the van brings you home. The forge smelts jewels into silver, the only money besides quest rewards and the overrun chest; silver buys gear from four merchants, and the locker holds what you own. Dying loses what you carried and what you took out, and nobody is left without a gun (the van issues a plain pistol). Quitting mid-trip counts as dying. A first-time player gets Campaign, and the last style chosen is remembered in prefs (`outbreak.style`, `src/game/style-pref.ts`). The two keep separate saves and bests: Campaign's home save and `outbreak.campaign-progress` against Arcade's `outbreak.level-progress`, and the best single trip (`outbreak.best-trip.v2`) against the best whole run (`outbreak.best.v2`). The design is in `docs/specs/2026-11-05-home-loop-design.md`.
- **Style in co-op**: a server runs one room with one simulation, so a room has one style. The first player into an empty room chooses it and everyone after plays it, even when it differs from their remembered choice, which is never overwritten. An invite link skips the Style step, so a joiner follows the room, and an empty room takes the joiner's remembered style. When the room empties it is free to change style.
- **Biomes**: every level grows its own kind of town from one table in `shared/biomes.ts`, picked by level (`generateWorldMap(seed, level)`, so the welcome packet carries the level). Level 1 is the plain town of oaks and a few pines; 2 is overgrown; 3 scrapland (bare dirt, cactus, dead trees, junk barricades across side streets); 4 marsh (reeds, flooded patches, a park pond, a drowned convoy); 5 blight (sludge and giant fungus); 6 pinewood; 7 a cordon (fenced side streets with a gate, checkpoints); 8 tundra (spruce and drifts); 9 the hive (fungus nests); 10 ashland (ash, a crash site, burning houses). A biome sets the plant species, the ground, how thick things grow, wrecks and rubble, an optional park layout (ring, pond, clearing, crater), one landmark in a park corner (`shared/landmarks.ts`), and walls or afflicted buildings in town (`shared/town-pieces.ts`).
- **Ground**: biomes lay flood water, snow, ash and sludge over the town in patches. They are walkable and slow anyone crossing them, zombies too, and change how far a sprinter's step carries (`shared/ground.ts`: water 70% speed and 1.5x noise, snow 88% and 0.7x, sludge 65% and 1.2x, ash 95% and 0.9x). Each ground has its own footstep sound (`src/audio/footsteps.ts`), played for you and for players within hearing range.
- **Street lamps** stand on every street corner and then at an even pitch between corners (`shared/town-streets.ts`); avenues light both sides in pairs, narrow streets stagger them. About a quarter are dead.
- **The town** is 3200x3200 pixels with five districts (Downtown, Riverside, Industrial Yard, Old Town, Park). It is generated from a seed and fenced in. Each building has a real room behind one south door, and the roof fades when you walk in.
- **Loot** sits in buildings by type: medical supplies in pharmacies and clinics, guns and ammo at the police station and gun shop. The carbine is always at the police station and the shotgun at the gun shop. Each gun takes its own ammo, and spare rounds have a cap. Some towns also have S.O.S. Discount, a downtown store (building kind `discount`) with cheap goods on its shelves and a back-room safe worth checking.
- **Zombies** roam the town from the start. They notice you by sight, which walls block, and by noise, which gunfire spreads.
- **Hordes** come after a scavenging lull: 140 seconds before the first and 100 seconds between the rest. A horde lasts up to 60 seconds, and any survivors go back to roaming.
- **Levels** run from 1 to 10, each its own town with its own look. A title card names the level and how many hordes it takes. Once the last one is seen off, a banner says the van has come, an amber arrow on screen and a marker on the town map point to it, and the HUD says to get there. Reduced motion stops the falling weather and the pulsing light.
- **The van ride**: in home mode, leaving the bunker and coming home play a 2.5-second night drive of the van across a letterboxed road, out left to right and home right to left to a stop and a door shutting, which covers the world being rebuilt; any key or click skips it, reduced motion shows the van standing still behind a plain fade, and it never holds up the game (`VanTransition.vue`, timing in `src/game/van-transition.ts`).
- **Searching**: hold E beside cars, boxes (crates), shelves, or furniture (drawers, lockers, fridges, cabinets) to go through them, from one second for a drawer to two for a car or locker. Moving or taking a hit undoes the search and it wants a fresh press. What was inside falls at your feet, a container stays open for everyone, and about one search in ten knocks something over, which zombies within 170 pixels hear. Most containers hold nothing, so searching stays a choice, but every home has a find in it somewhere. The ammo budget counts floors and containers together, and every town settles on it with half in each, topped up or trimmed at every level and difficulty. Containers follow from the world map and interiors in `shared/containers.ts`, their contents in `shared/container-loot.ts` and the rules in `shared/container-search.ts`.
- **The Radio** is a find in a searched container: about one car in ten still has a working one, and some towns also hold one, never more than one, left in a house, apartment, diner or garage. None takes the place of ammo. Which containers follow from the town's seed alone, on a generator of its own (`shared/radio.ts`), so the simulation's rolls do not move. Any pickup plays a short static-and-tone sound and gives up the next track of the level it was found in that this browser has not yet found (a banner names it); once a level has none left, a Radio is only the sound. In co-op the pickup event reaches every connected client, so the whole party unlocks together. The finds are saved apart from the level progress (`src/game/sound-unlock.ts`), survives reloads and old saves, and the simulation never reads it. Finding a radio unlocks the **Radio** option in the title menu's Extras.
- **The Radio (Extras)**: finding a radio unlocks the **Radio** option under Extras on the title menu (`RadioPage.vue`). It opens an authentic in-game pixel art boombox overlay (`RadioDeck.vue`) across the top with olive chassis, stereo speakers, lit FM tuner dial with animated red needle, cassette bay with rotating spools, and tactile tape deck transport buttons with crisp pixel icons and zero text labels. A full-width tape playlist (`RadioPlaylist.vue`) sits underneath with All Tapes, Favorites, and Radio Finds filters, live search, and per-track favorite toggles persisted to browser storage (`outbreak.radio-favorites`). Continuous media player playback advances through unlocked tracks with Repeat (Off, All, One) and Shuffle (`src/game/radio-player.ts`).
- **Sound Test**: always open from the title menu. It lists the music by name for levels reached and ??? for the rest, and for each level's radio finds (every take after its first) ??? until a Radio has given it up, with a count of finds so far (the title and horde tracks belong to level 1, and the victory and credits tracks open on finishing the game), plus every sound effect. Lists are read from `src/audio/playlists.ts` and the sound table, so a new track or sound shows up on its own. One thing plays at a time: select to play, select again or Stop to stop. Music goes through the soundtrack and mixer, so volume and mute apply, and the title music returns when the page closes. The lists and unlock rules are in `src/game/sound-test.ts`. The bunker's **Jukebox** station (quarters, hold E; `JukeboxScreen.vue`) plays any track already heard or found through the same preview path, and shows the radio-find count.
- **Town rules**: three themes change play as well as the look, from one table in `shared/town-rules.ts`, and the level card names the rule. Floodwater (level 4) slows walking outdoors to 80%, buildings stay dry. Blackout (level 6) cuts how far zombies see to 70% but every noise carries 30% further. Fresh snow (level 8) keeps a print every 9 ticks of walking or sprinting outdoors for 20 seconds, and a roamer that crosses one follows it to whoever left it; sneaking leaves none (`shared/snow-tracks.ts`).
- **Bottles and car alarms**: everyone starts a run with one empty bottle and can carry three. G throws one at the crosshair, up to 170 pixels and short of the first wall; it breaks at once and every zombie within 220 pixels of the smash comes to look, which pulls them off you. An empty-handed search turns up a bottle 35% of the time. Going through a car sets its alarm off one time in eight, a 420-pixel noise at the car. Rules in `shared/bottles.ts`.
- **Boss nights** (`shared/bosses.ts`): levels 3, 6 and 9 (`BOSS_LEVELS`). `maybeSummonBoss` runs as each horde starts and, on the town's last horde, places the boss on its own generator 420 to 700 pixels from a survivor: a zombie of its base type with `boss` set, its radius and reach scaled, its own health, damage and points, and a `bossState` clock. `updateBosses` runs its trick each tick (Howler: a 700-pixel scream plus two runners; Mother: three walkers; Butcher: a charge when someone is within 260 pixels). The horde's clock cannot end it while the boss lives (`liveBoss`), headshots skip it, and `bossDown` drops a medkit and a prize. The snapshot carries `boss` (id and health) for the HUD bar, and the renderer draws the sprite at the boss's scale.
- **Night events** (`shared/night-events.ts`): from level 2, 30% of towns (never the bunker) draw an event from the map seed: fog (zombie sight x0.55), a blood moon (hordes x2 through `waveRoster`'s `eventScale`, kill points x2, the headshot bonus not doubled) or a power cut (sight x0.7, hearing x1.3; a blackout town gets fog instead). The sim reads it through `eventOf(sim)`, cached per town. The client derives it from `snapshot.mapSeed`, so the level card names it and `withEvent` in `src/engine/level-look.ts` lays it over the town's look.
- **Kill chains** (`shared/kill-chain.ts`): kills by one survivor within 1.4 seconds of each other chain, the same window the client's DOUBLE/TRIPLE callouts use. The nth kill in a chain scores its points times 1 + 0.25 per kill past the first, capped at 2; `zombie_die` carries `chain`, the points actually paid, and `chainPaid` when the chain changed them.
- **Kill points** (`shared/kill-points.ts`): score is the XP a run earned, one for one. A kill pays `round(worth × town × kill chain × night event / 10)`, where worth is the type's `points` in `ZOMBIES` or `BOSSES` and town is the town's number, so a walker pays 1 in town 1 and 10 in town 10. The bunker has no number and pays as the furthest town reached (`townOf`). A headshot pays 1 × town more, once, on the shot that kills (`payHeadshot` in `shared/headshots.ts`): a head hit that does not kill emits `headshot` with `points: 0`, the headshot streak multiplies nothing, and night events never touch the bonus.
- **Levels and AP** (`shared/progression.ts`, the save in `shared/progression-save.ts`): every survivor has their own level, XP, AP, owed AP, pips and upkeep clock, so picking someone else shows their level, not the last one's. The XP a run earns goes to the survivor played; it levels them up to 100, level L costing `round(40 × L^1.5)` XP, and each level pays 1 AP, a pip on that survivor's stat bar. Pips are display only: the sim never reads them. Upkeep is one level a week per survivor: each full week past that survivor's last level-up takes one pip (banked AP first, then their fullest stat, and they go Rusty) and owes it back, a level-up repaying one owed AP on top of its own. The level never drops. `checkApUpkeep` charges every survivor on the title, setup and Nightly Run screens and at run start, never on a kill. At level 100 nothing decays and that survivor reads as maxed, worked out from the level. A solo run hosted from the Pause menu is the same run, so it is not charged again. The save (`outbreak.progression.v3`) is a book `{ version: 3, survivors: { 0..4: state } }`. Saves from before per-survivor levels (`outbreak.progression`, `.v2`) are not read: everyone starts over. Dev: Test Drive runs bank no XP, hosted or not: the room's welcome carries `testDrive`.
- **Nightly Run** (`src/game/daily-night.ts`): one town a day, the same for everyone. The local date is hashed (FNV-1a) into a seed, a start level from 2 to 8 and a difficulty (mostly Lights Out, sometimes kinder or harder). The run is solo with Arcade rules; the first run of the day counts and the rest are practice. The title menu's Nightly Run page shows the town, your result, your streak and a countdown to midnight, and each counted day can be copied as a short result to share (`shareText`), and is kept on the device for the streak and the Records. The log lives under `outbreak.daily.v2`. Days played before level 100 are carried over from `outbreak.daily` for the streak, with their kills, town and attempts but no points (`carried`); the day of the change is not carried, so its first run on the new scale counts.
- **Supply drops** (`shared/supply-drop.ts`): once a town, 12 seconds into the lull after the first horde, a flare goes up on open ground 320 to 900 pixels from the nearest survivor (a lit flare in `sim.lures`, so it hisses all the while) and a banner says SUPPLY DROP. Ten seconds later the crate lands with a 450-pixel crash and leaves a round kit, two double boxes of ammo, a medkit, and a large jewel on a Campaign trip or a gun part in Arcade. The spot and contents come from the town's seed on their own generator, so the sim's rolls do not move. The snapshot carries `drop` (spot and whether it landed) from the flare until 40 seconds after landing, and the client points to it with a red arrow on screen and a red box on the town map.
- **Fireworks** (`shared/throwables.ts`): searching can turn up a spinner firework (garages 30%, warehouses 20%, diners 12% of containers) or a fountain firework (police 25%, garages 12%, cars 10%), carried two of each. Which containers hide them follows from the town's seed and the container's index on a generator of its own, so the sim's rolls do not move. T lights a firework (no bottle) and tosses it up to 150 pixels, where it lands as a ground spinner (`sim.spinners`): a 240-pixel fizz, then sparks 30 pixels round it for 5 seconds that light every zombie in them and burn anyone standing in them, players too, 4 every half second. Popping Eyeballs widen the sparks by half; the Pocket Lighter keepsake makes them spin half as long again. V lights a fountain and tosses it up to 190 pixels, where it sprays gold sparks up for 20 seconds, hissing (a 260-pixel noise every 1.5 seconds) like the supply drop's flare, and sets alight any zombie that comes within 20 pixels (`sim.lures`, `sparks`). Both share the bottle's 0.6-second cooldown, and fireworks are dropped when the van leaves town. The client draws the spinner (four colored spouts turning, `src/engine/fx-director.ts`) and the flare from their events alone (`spinner_lit`, `fountain_lit`); the drawing lives in `src/engine/burning-fx.ts`.
- **Screamers** roam from level 3 (3% of roamers, half a percent more each level, never in a horde). They are walkers recoloured grey and violet, frail (45 health) and worth 3 times the town. One that spots a survivor wails, a noise every zombie within 300 pixels hears, then needs 10 seconds before it can call again (`scream` in `shared/zombie-senses.ts`).
- **Sneak and sprint**: hold C to sneak at 55% speed, spotted from 60% as far; hold Shift to sprint 40% faster, with footsteps heard 110 pixels away every half second. Sprinting costs stamina (`shared/stamina.ts`): a baseline survivor runs a full bar dry in 5 seconds, scaled by their Speed stat (Kat lasts 12% longer). The bar refills in 4 seconds after half a second of rest, and a survivor who ran dry walks until it is back to a quarter. The HUD shows the Sprint bar whenever it is not full.
- **Out of ammo?** Shove. It makes no noise and pushes back the two nearest zombies. A zombie you just shoved keeps its footing for a moment, so standing still and shoving will not hold off a crowd or a brute.
- **The knife** turns up in diners, houses, garages and containers. With one the shove becomes Shove & Stab: 18 damage instead of 4, just as silent, with the same two-target limit and stagger immunity. It comes with you from town to town and is lost when you die.
- **Survivors**: pick one of five on the title screen, and the choice is remembered. Night moves quieter (shots, sprinting and search clatter carry three quarters as far, and a search clatters half as often) and is spotted from three quarters as far, Bear reloads in three quarters of the time, Kat runs 12% faster, Rex shoves half again as hard, and Kai's kills drop loot twice as often, with about one empty container in seven turning something up anyway. The traits are one table in `shared/characters.ts`. In co-op nobody shares a survivor: you get yours if it is free, otherwise the next free one, and teammates' tags and HUD chips name who they are playing.
- **Co-op** keeps your run if you refresh: you come back with the same health, guns and spot, and a dead teammate still waits for the next horde. A newcomer after the first horde starts with half health and no spares. If the last player standing leaves, even to refresh, the run ends. Play Again restarts once everyone has asked, or 15 seconds after the first person asked.
- **Difficulty** has four modes, picked on the setup page and remembered: Night Light On, Stay Close (the default), Lights Out and Undead Tired. Each scales the ammo in town, zombie speed and bite, horde size and the lull between hordes. Lights Out is the balance the game was tuned on. In co-op the first player into an empty room sets it, and the results show it. The table is `shared/difficulty.ts`.
- **The workbench** opens after each van ride but the last. Barrels, grips, magazines and suppressors from the gun shop, police station, garages and warehouses buy upgrades within a gun's slots: damage, fire rate, magazine size, reload speed, or a suppressor that cuts its noise radius by 65%. A rare toolkit moves one upgrade between guns. A found gun can carry a quirk, good and bad at once. Upgrades stay on the gun across levels and go with it on death, and no build passes 1.5 times stock damage per second. Solo waits until you are ready; co-op gives everyone on their feet 45 seconds.
- **Special rounds** are a separate slot from upgrades: a gun holds at most one effect. Round kits (tins with a mark on the lid) turn up mostly on floors and sometimes in searched containers, three or four a town, and a player carries at most two of each. Fitting one at the workbench uses it and replaces any effect already on the gun, after a "Replace X with Y?" confirm; a toolkit moves an effect to a gun that has none. *Flame rounds* set a zombie alight for 3 seconds (18 damage), let a burning zombie light one that touches it, and the fire is loud and glows. *Acid rounds* stack corrosion up to 3 times for 5 seconds. A shotgun blast adds one stack however many pellets land, and each stack makes that zombie take 10% more damage (15% on a brute) from acid rounds and from burns, not from plain guns, so a teammate's acid never lifts another gun past the power cap. *Piercing rounds* go through the first zombie into the next at half damage, and walls still stop them. *Tranq rounds* slow a zombie for 3 seconds (less on brutes) and hit 30% lighter. Effects only apply to ranged hits, credit the shooter, and the gun's direct damage gives way so no build passes the power cap. Status lives on the zombie and clears with the level; carried guns keep their effect. The HUD names the active gun's effect next to its ammo, and the gun's pips show a coloured effect pip. On screen a burning zombie shows pixel flames and a glow, an acid-hit one a green wash with drips, a slowed one a blue wash, and effect rounds leave a coloured tracer trail. Reduced motion freezes the flames and drips, and flames are capped per frame (`MAX_FLAMES`).
- **Starting at a level** is Arcade: on the Levels page (always in the title menu, with a note that it is Arcade's page), a level you have reached can be picked to start a solo Arcade run there, and picking one skips the Style step. The setup page then shows "Start at level N: title", and a level that is not unlocked, a stale saved value or an unknown number falls back to a normal run from level 1. Continue from level N is Arcade only: it sits beside Start when Arcade is the style and a level has been reached, and Campaign has no such button because it resumes from its bunker save. Arcade co-op always starts on level 1, and the setup page says so; Campaign co-op picks its level at the garage map. The rules are in `src/game/level-progress.ts` and the words in `src/game/level-select.ts`.
- **The camp** is Campaign's home (`shared/bunker-map.ts` builds it; the code keeps the name bunker, so `isBunkerMap`, `BUNKER_SEED` and the `'bunker'` theme still mean home). A 72x48-tile map of grass and dirt: a scrap fence of tin, pallets and chain-link round three tents, wilderness outside. The fence is its first props, in ring order, so section `i` is `map.props[i]` (`shared/camp/camp-ring.ts`); it stops bodies but not shots or sight (`MOVEMENT_ONLY_PROPS`, `segmentHitsCover` in `shared/world-query.ts`). The tents are buildings (`camp_market`, `camp_workshop`, `camp_quarters` in `BUILDING_DEFS`, art in `src/art/environment/camp-tents.ts`): walked into by their doors, walls that stop shots, roofs that fade, nothing to loot. The candy shop is in the market, the bench in the workshop, the locker and jukebox in the quarters, the map board and chest outside by the gate and van, with a spot kept beside the map board for the Roster (`shared/camp/camp-layout.ts`). The camp has five sizes, one per town the room has reached (`campSizeFor` in `shared/camp/camp-latch.ts`), fixed when the room comes home or when a fresh room's first player walks in, and held for the visit (`HomeRun.campSize`, `campLevel`). The fence grows west, north and south; nothing inside the smallest ring moves. The size travels as `campSize` on the welcome and the snapshot, left off at size 1 and in towns, so a guest builds the very camp the room plays (protocol 6).
- **The people to find** (protocol 7; `shared/camp/rescuee.ts`, `rescuee-gun.ts`). A new campaign starts with an empty camp. Towns 1 to 4 each hold one merchant (Sarge, Mags, Doc, Cinder) while anyone on the trip has not rescued them (`RESCUE_BY_LEVEL`; placement uses its own `RESCUE_SALT` generator). Hold E, standing still, to take them; they follow on their own flow field, and count as rescued if they are within `BOARD_RADIUS` of the van as it leaves (`rescued` on the banked receipt, kept in `HomeSave.camp.rescued`). The camp is `1 + people rescued` in the room, latched like its size (`campSizeFor`, `unionRescued`); a stall, its quests and marks open only for a save that rescued them ("WE HAVEN'T MET" otherwise). A rescue takes up the person's starter quest; saves with trips on file start with all four (`migrateCamp`). Hold E beside a following person with a spare gun to lend it: they hold fire until you shoot or something hunts, and it returns to your gear at the van. Doc and Cinder name a stash when freed (`sim.clues`). The breach horde is `hordeShare(people)` of full. Not built yet: zombies targeting them, downed/revive, seats, the Roster (see tickets under #7556).
- **The Radio** is the jukebox station (id `jukebox`), dead until the player's save has `camp.radio` (`JoinHome.radio`; `stationHere` in `shared/stations.ts`). A radio found credits everyone in the room on their receipt (`radioLevels`), and town 2 always holds one until the room has them.
- **The overrun** is Campaign's camp clock, live for everyone since 2026-11-07 (`OVERRUN_LIVE` in `shared/home-protocol.ts`; set it back to false to hide it again, and a dev build then still turns it on with `?overrun`). A co-op server follows `OVERRUN_LIVE` alone (`overrunOn` in `src/game/home-mode.ts`). How to Play has a chapter on it. Each arrival in the bunker starts a clock whose length follows the run's difficulty (`OVERRUN_CLOCK_BY_DIFFICULTY`: 240 seconds on Night Light On, 180 on Stay Close, 150 on Lights Out and 120 on Undead Tired; the span travels to clients as `OverrunView.spanSec`) that runs at `OVERRUN_MENU_RATE` (half speed) while everyone who counts has a station menu open, and stops under the solo pause menu. Rising warnings lead to a breach. On the tick the clock arms (the last warning, guns free) the sim picks 1 to 4 fence sections with its own rolls (`gapCount` and `pickGaps` in `shared/camp/breach-pick.ts`: one below level 4, two to level 6, three from level 7, one more on Lights Out and Undead Tired; spread round the ring), lists them as `OverrunView.gaps`, and emits `fence_rattle` at each every 1.5 seconds; at zero it emits `fence_fall`, lifts them out of collision (`settleFence` in `shared/camp/fence-fall.ts`, which every client also runs from the view) and deals a horde sized by the latched level (at most `OVERRUN_HORDE_MAX`, 140) round-robin to open ground 4 to 7 tiles outside each gap. Kill it and every player opens a reward chest; run for the van and merchants die (never the last stall) and the rest thin out for 3 banked trips; go down and lose the gear in hand, while the camp spares the merchants. Only bodies in the room when the van leaves pay for a flight; one that left from the van before the breach owes nothing, and a refresh in the breach rejoins empty-handed. What can be opened where (the chest for a player whose chest is waiting, no stall while the horde is in) is one rule, `openableStation` in `shared/stations.ts`, shared by the sim and the client's HOLD E prompt. No clock runs below `OVERRUN_FIRST_HIGHEST` (level 2). The rules are in `shared/overrun.ts`, `shared/overrun-fight.ts` and `shared/overrun-chest.ts`, the client side in `src/game/home/overrun-ledger.ts` and `overrun-merchants.ts`, and the design in `docs/specs/2026-11-05-home-loop-design.md`. `tests/overrun-soak.spec.ts` prints a bot's clear rate and minutes per visit at highest 1, 5 and 10 on every difficulty, to tune the constants from (run it with `chemx test tests/overrun-soak.spec.ts --raw`). It adds no analytics event.
- **Solo** pauses when the window loses focus.
- **Kill beats**: your kills get a short hit-stop in solo (headshot, chain, brute), chain callouts (DOUBLE, TRIPLE, QUAD, MASSACRE xN) within 1.4 seconds, CLOSE CALL at 20% health or less, and HORDE WIPED OUT with a held frame and a flash when the last of a horde falls. It reads snapshot events only (`src/game/kill-juice.ts`), and the solo hold uses `LocalSession.hold`.

## Architecture

- **Simulation**: `shared/` holds the deterministic `GameSimulation` at 30 ticks a second. The browser runs it directly for solo, and the server runs the same code for co-op. Town generation is in `shared/world-map.ts` and the `town-*` files, loot in `shared/loot.ts`, zombie senses in `shared/zombie-senses.ts`, gun upgrades in `shared/gun-upgrade-data.ts` and `shared/gun-upgrades.ts`, and the hold between towns in `shared/workbench.ts`. A `GameSimulation` only stops at the bench when its `workbench` option says so: solo passes `hold`, the server `timed`.
- **Server**: `server/server.ts` is an authoritative `ws` server. Each client gets its own snapshot from `shared/snapshot-view.ts`, with zombies and pickups within `VIEWER_RANGE` (600px) of that player, or around every player for a dead viewer. Close codes: 4000 room full (or, to a client from before styles, a Campaign room it cannot play), 4001 server busy, 4002 no join within 10 seconds. On connection, before any join, the server sends `{ type: 'room', style }`: the room's style, or null for an empty room, so a Campaign client can bring its home with its join and an Arcade client does not. The join carries the style; an empty room takes it, and a join naming another style is not admitted and hears the room packet again, so the client rejoins with the right gear. A join with no style is an old client and counts as Arcade. Deploy the server before the client: a new client against an old server waits for a room packet that never comes. A link too slow for the snapshots falls behind rather than catching up.
- **Client**: `src/game/` owns the run and the HUD state, `src/engine/` renders to a low-resolution back buffer (`BuildingLayer` for shells and rooms, `LightField` for the dithered lighting), and `src/network/` holds the local and socket sessions, with snapshot interpolation and local prediction. `src/game/run-stage.ts` watches each snapshot's `mapSeed` and `level`: a new town rebuilds the world, renderer, town map and prediction before that snapshot's events run, and `src/engine/level-look.ts` holds each level's light, tint and weather.
- **Art**: `src/art/` builds every sprite as pure RGBA data, with no image files. Render contact sheets with `node --experimental-strip-types tools/contact-sheet.ts <sheet> out.png` (`kits` shows the four round kits as pickups and icons). The link preview image `public/og-image.png` is rendered from the night scene and the title logo with `node --experimental-strip-types tools/share-image.ts`. Status drawing lives in `src/engine/status-fx.ts` and works from the snapshot alone, so nothing lingers across a reconnect; the effect sparks are in `status-bursts.ts` and the sound recipes in `src/audio/sfx-effects.ts`. The workbench rows for effects are in `src/game/bench-effects.ts` and `WorkbenchEffect.vue`.
- **Survivor looks and costumes**: a survivor is a `Person` (`src/art/survivors/rig.ts`): a legend, tones and hand-drawn grids the rig bakes into a 40x38 cell with a selective outline. Each survivor has a costume list in `src/art/survivors/costumes/<name>.ts`, default first, and `shared/characters.ts` repeats the ids and names (`costumesOf(skin)`); `tests/costumes-wiring.spec.ts` keeps the two in step. `SurvivorArt.costumes[0]` is the survivor's own record and the others are built on first read. Costume ids are what a saved unlock will name, so they do not change once shipped. Nothing wears a non-default costume yet: the player and the protocol carry only `skin`. Render them with `tools/contact-sheet.ts costumes out.png 4 night/aurora asphalt`. Fills never use the outline ink (it sinks into the road), and a new look should be judged on the `asphalt` ground, not the flat sheet.
- **Audio**: `src/audio/` synthesizes every sound effect, the wind and the distant groans with Web Audio. Music is recorded tracks, all by Majora D., in `public/music/` (128-192 kbps MP3), which `soundtrack.ts` streams through the mixer: *Silent Ruins (Variation)* on the title, the lull of each town from `playlists.ts` (a level's first take is its own; the rest are radio finds that join the rotation once found; it starts on a random one of what is found, then plays the rest in turn, across all 10 levels), and *Horde Attack* and its alternate in turn for hordes. Game over has no music. The Sound Test plays tracks through `Soundtrack.audition()` and sounds through `AudioEngine.previewSfx()`. A won run plays *Dark Salvation* on `VictoryScreen.vue`, then `CreditsScreen.vue` (lines in `src/game/credits.ts`) rolls to *Surviving the Night*, and any key or click skips to the title; `EndingFlow.vue` runs the two. If a track will not load, the old generated music takes over. `event-sounds.ts` maps each game event to its sounds. Doors and boards (open, close, nailed, pried, pounded, broken) play where the door stands. An emote chirps for the whole party at a pitch of its own, and the sender hears it louder than everyone else. An overrun ends on the cleared-wave sound when the bunker holds and on its own sting when it falls (`sfx-world.ts`). Each salvage, supply and candy pickup has its own sound (`sfx-salvage.ts`); food and water end on a short chime, as the medkit does.
- **UI**: `src/components/` holds the studio splash, title screen, HUD, banners, town map and game over screen. Any key or click skips the splash, and `?nosplash` in the address turns it off. The title is the logo, `TitleMenu.vue` and the credit; the items and the words on its pages live in `src/game/title-menu.ts`. Each page sits in `TitlePage.vue`, which gives it the panel, heading, Back and Esc, and the title scales on its own unit, `--title-u`. The Levels item is always in the menu: it is Arcade's page. The Play page has four steps (`src/game/play-flow.ts`), all laid out like the survivor select: Style, Campaign or Arcade (`StyleSelect.vue`, facts from `style-select-view.ts`, card art in `src/art/styles/` and `style-cards.ts`, placeholders until they are painted), which a pick remembers but does not start; then Solo or Multiplayer (`ModeSelect.vue`, facts from `mode-select-view.ts`) with the callsign and, for co-op, the server field and invite link; then the difficulty (`DifficultySelect.vue`, meters from `difficulty-meters.ts`); then Choose Survivor (`CharacterSelect.vue`, `SurvivorRoster.vue`, `StatBars.vue`), which holds the Start button. The Style step is skipped when the style is already settled: starting from the Levels page means Arcade, and an invite link lets the room decide, so those walks have three steps (`startFlow`). The first three share `SceneCard.vue` (a framed scene as tall as a survivor bust, nameplate over its foot, stacked rows beside it) and `SceneRoster.vue` (the tiles to pick from), with the art on `ui.style`, `ui.mode` and `ui.difficulty`. Each survivor's five stat bars come from `shared/character-stats.ts`, derived from `shared/characters.ts`, and the large picture is a hand-drawn bust in `src/art/survivors/busts/`. Enter in a field only moves to the next step; a run starts from Start or Join on the survivor step. Back or Esc during a connection cancels it. The title shows the app version from `package.json` bottom-left.
- **The HUD and the touch buttons**: `HudOverlay.vue` is click-through except where a slot takes a press. Top left is the town and the horde; top centre, on a Campaign trip, the treasure and quest chips; top right the three numbers a run is read by, in order: health (heart, bar, number, blinking red under a third), kills behind `art.ui.zombieHead`, then points in amber, with co-op's Linked / Signal weak line under them. What is carried stays bottom left. The gun bar is pinned to the middle of the foot of the screen at every width, and each slot is a button that sends its number key through `input.padDown`/`padUp` (`src/game/weapon-bar.ts`); a finger gets a `--tap` target, a mouse keeps the small chip and the key hints. The run log reads down the right edge, halfway down. On touch, Search, the backpack, Shove and Sprint sit two by two in the bottom-right corner as the HUD's own pixel chips (`MobileFAB.vue`, `TouchControls.vue`), Sprint in the corner held like `ShiftLeft`; held upright the gun bar lifts above that block (`--cluster`, `touch-ui.css`). The backpack opens a ring on the survivor: `src/game/backpack-wheel.ts` says what it holds, drawn with the game's own sprites (medkit, the gun `Q` hands over to, each throwable carried, the candy and the town map), a slot greyed out where the pick would do nothing, and Esc, a tap outside or picking anything closes it. The ring follows the survivor through `--player-x` and `--player-y`, which `src/game/run-frame.ts` writes onto `<html>` once a frame from `src/game/player-anchor.ts`; the HUD's own state is only rebuilt ten times a second, which is far too slow to sit on a walking survivor, and its slot art is sized off the chip rather than off `--u`, which would draw the 32-wide gun icon far outside it. On a desktop a middle-click opens the same ring and the scroll wheel steps its highlight instead of cycling guns (`stepSlot`, caught in the capture phase so the game's own wheel listener never sees it). `backpackOpen` lives in `UiState` so both doors open one ring, gated on `standingInTown` (`src/game/hud-state.ts`) and re-checked on every publish, so pausing, the map, the workbench, going down or the run ending closes it from wherever the news arrives; nothing can start a shot while it is up, though the survivor keeps walking.
- **How to Play** is a printed magazine, *Out of Town*, in `src/components/magazine/`, with the Town Survival Bureau as its publisher. `src/game/how-to-play.ts` holds the rules and stays the only source of them. `magazine-pages.ts` builds the issue from those sections, `magazine-hints.ts` says how each section is laid out, and `how-to-play-visuals.ts` and `magazine-photos.ts` supply the pictures, posing the game's own sprites on its own tiles (`town` is a real clip of a generated town). A section with no hint gets the default spread, and a point a hint does not place is added to its first article, so new rules always print. `magazine-fit.ts` keeps a page from overflowing: it shrinks the type, then drops blocks marked `extra`, then shrinks again. `magazine-controller.ts` handles turning: Prev and Next, chapter links, the arrow and page keys, and a swipe, with a 3D page turn or a plain swap under reduced motion. A window taller than it is wide reads one page at a time. The issue has a chapter on the two styles and six on Campaign (bunker, trips, treasure, forge, merchants, dying), hinted in `magazine-hints.ts` with the stations, treasure and merchants drawn from the art book. Twenty chapters share the thirteen page layouts, so a layout can return but never next door, and a contents page of more than fourteen chapters drops the italic kicker under each title so they all fit.
- **Records** is a printed record book on the How to Play paper (`RecordsPage.vue`, `src/components/records/`, page plan in `src/game/record-book.ts`): personal bests for each style (Campaign's best single trip, Arcade's best whole run), a ledger of the top five whose rows say where they came from ("Campaign trip" for an entry the trip-end recorder marked, "Arcade run" otherwise), the Nightly Run streak and best night, the last seven nights stamped Counted, Missed or Tonight, and two leaves of achievement stamps (`src/game/stamps.ts`), each inked with the day it was earned. Two pages show at a time on a wide screen, one on a tall one, and each page shrinks its type to fit like a magazine page (`recordSections` and `recordRows` in `src/game/title-menu.ts`). Personal bests, the ledger, the best trip and the daily ghosts started again at level 100 under `.v2` storage keys, because points fell to a tenth times the town; the old entries stay in storage and are not shown.
- **The map** (Tab or N) has its own key in the camp: "Camp map", with Stations as its one colour, and none of the town's legend or check and cross marks (`src/game/map-key.ts`). It draws the tents at the camp's scale, the fence ring and every gap the horde has made (`src/game/map/camp-map.ts`).
- **Analytics** live in `src/game/analytics/` and are on by default, with no prompt. `index.html` has no Google tag: `sender.ts` injects it at runtime unless the player turned Analytics off in Options. A saved off stays off, and a Global Privacy Control signal counts as off (Options shows it off and disabled). The choice is saved apart from the other saves (`outbreak.analytics-consent`); old saves with no answer count as on. A small Privacy link and the version number sit above the title's copyright line, and Options links to the same Privacy page (`privacy-text.ts`). Turning it off sets Google's `ga-disable` flag and clears the `_ga` cookies where the browser allows (`cookies.ts`). Nothing is sent from localhost, 127.0.0.1 or a dev build, which log `[analytics] would send` instead. `catalog.ts` is the allow-list of events and parameters: a value not on its list is dropped, and page addresses are sent without their query. One listener (`listener.ts`, started from `App.vue`) feeds `run-tracker.ts`, which turns screens, the HUD and the game's events into one summary per level or run. The game reaches it only through three one-line taps (`event-tap.ts`, called from `run-hud.ts` and `bench-link.ts`), which cannot change play. Title pages are counted by `TITLE_PAGE_EVENTS` in `run-tracker.ts`: add a page there and call `trackTitlePage` when it opens. The words of the Privacy page are in `privacy-text.ts` and must change with any change to what is sent; they have not been reviewed by anyone qualified. Choosing a style adds no event or parameter. A Campaign run reaches the same events differently (read from `run-tracker.ts`, not observed in analytics output): `run_start` fires on entering the bunker with `start_level` 0, `level_start` fires once for each trip so a level can repeat, `level_complete` never fires because a trip does not go through the next-town step, `run_end` only ever says quit or disconnected and one pair spans a whole bunker session of many trips, and `level_reached` is the last trip's level or 0. The Privacy page's wording has not been reviewed against this.
- **Install and offline**: the game is an installable app (`public/manifest.webmanifest`, icons drawn by `tools/pwa-icons.ts` from the same cartridge as Install Game, its label reading UNDEAD OF and NIGHT, filling the icon with a transparent background (the maskable and Apple icons stay on the ink colour, since they must be opaque), with no studio name on them; the favicon stays the zombie head, and `tests/pwa-assets.spec.ts` fails if the PNGs are out of date with the generator). Every path is relative, so it also works under a subfolder. The service worker is the hand-written `public/sw.js`, registered only in a production build (`src/game/pwa/worker-update.ts`), never on a dev server. `tools/vite-pwa.ts` stamps it at build time with a build id and the list of built files, so each build is a new worker and old caches are removed on activate. The shell is precached, so solo play opens offline; pages go network first with the cached page as fallback, and hashed assets are cache first. Music is not precached: a track is kept the first time it plays (at most 12, oldest dropped, stored one at a time so concurrent first plays cannot overshoot), range requests are answered from the kept copy (a multi-range request gets the whole file), a kept track is keyed by the build's hash of its file (`MUSIC_VERSIONS`, stamped by `tools/vite-pwa.ts`) so a replaced file is fetched again while unchanged tracks stay kept and are pruned on activate when replaced or removed, only a URL without a query is kept in the shell cache, before a waiting version takes over the page tells the worker in charge to abort its music streams (`RELEASE_MUSIC`) so a paused stream cannot hold the swap up for minutes, and a track that was never kept just fails and the game plays on with its generated bed. Only same-origin GET requests are touched, so Google Analytics and co-op sockets pass straight through. A new version waits and is applied only while the title screen shows, never during a run, or at the next launch; the title's state is remembered (`createUpdateHost`), so it counts whether the title mounted before or after the worker started, and an update on a first visit reloads too. Install Game is a pixel cartridge beside the main menu under the title (`InstallCartridge.vue`, named Install Game by its `aria-label`). The whole 48 by 56 cartridge, label included, is painted in the master palette by `src/art/cartridge-art.ts` and kept as flat SVG rectangles by `src/art/cartridge.ts`: a grey shell with a ridged grip and gold contacts, and a label (INSTALL and GAME on the button, `drawCartridge` takes other words for the app icons) with an INSTALL ribbon, GAME in bone capitals dipped in blood, the logo's moon and bat, a survivor whose flashlight finds a zombie in the street, and a gold Z seal. `src/game/pwa/install-offer.ts` decides when it shows: only when the browser can install and the game is not installed. Chrome, Edge and Android use the held `beforeinstallprompt`; iPhone and iPad Safari open a short magazine-style card (`IosInstallCard.vue`, words in `ios-guide.ts`) for Share, then Add to Home Screen. A dismissed prompt keeps the cartridge, which then opens a card pointing at the browser menu. The cartridge is out of the flow (so the menu never jumps when the prompt arrives) and is hidden on screens too short to hold it clear of the menu and the credit line; the menu's Enter and arrow keys leave a focused control outside the menu, such as the cartridge, to answer for itself. It never shows during a run, and analytics sends `install_prompt` and `app_installed` through `catalog.ts`.

## Running locally

1. Install dependencies from the repository root:
   ```bash
   pnpm install
   ```

2. Start the client (default port 8096; set `ZOMBIE_CLIENT_PORT` to change it):
   ```bash
   pnpm --filter zombie-survival dev
   ```

3. For co-op, also start the server (default port 8097; set `ZOMBIE_PORT` to change it):
   ```bash
   pnpm --filter zombie-survival dev:server
   ```

   `ZOMBIE_PORT` must be the same value for the client and the server: the client uses it as the default server address on the setup page. To run on other ports, for example 8196 and 8197:
   ```bash
   ZOMBIE_CLIENT_PORT=8196 ZOMBIE_PORT=8197 pnpm --filter zombie-survival dev
   ZOMBIE_PORT=8197 pnpm --filter zombie-survival dev:server
   ```

4. Open `http://localhost:8096` (or your `ZOMBIE_CLIENT_PORT`). Choose Solo, or enter the server address and choose Co-op in each browser window.
