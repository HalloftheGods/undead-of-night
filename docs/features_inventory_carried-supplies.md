# Carried Survival Supplies

## Overview
Survival supplies (`bandage`, `canned_food`, `water_bottle`, `disinfectant`, `antibiotics`) are carried grid items rather than instant-use pickups. They integrate with the player bag, auto-use from pockets when needed, support manual use and dropping via inventory actions, and persist across banked trips into the bunker locker.

## Core Mechanics

### 1. Pickup and Bag Storage
* Walking over a supply item automatically stores it into the player's carried supplies.
* No individual numerical caps exist; inventory grid space is the sole limit.
* If the player's pack and pockets have no free slot, the supply remains on the ground.
* Supplies clear on player death or run reset, matching medkit behavior.

### 2. Auto-Consumption from Pockets
Each simulation tick evaluates supplies held in the 2x2 pocket grid via `usePocketSupplies(p)`:
* **Bandage:** Used when player is bleeding, or health is at or below 50% (`POCKET_BANDAGE_SHARE = 0.5`). Heals missing HP (up to max HP) and binds open bleeding wounds.
* **Canned Food:** Consumed when hunger reaches or exceeds 60 (`POCKET_EAT_AT = 60`).
* **Water Bottle:** Consumed when thirst reaches or exceeds 60 (`POCKET_DRINK_AT = 60`).
* **Disinfectant:** Applied when infected and wound type is a scratch or laceration.
* **Antibiotics:** Taken when infection progression reaches or exceeds 35 (`POCKET_ANTIBIOTICS_AT = 35`).
* Pack items are never auto-consumed; only pocketed items trigger automated survival responses.

### 3. Manual Bag Actions (USE and DROP)
* In the inventory modal, holding an item displays action buttons on `HeldItemPanel.vue`:
  * **USE:** Uses the item immediately if applicable, consuming one unit from carried supplies.
  * **DROP:** Leaves the item on the ground as a world pickup.
* The item in hand is drawn on the game canvas with its buttons under the survivor (`src/engine/held-ui.ts`). Clicking the survivor uses it; clicking bare ground drops it.
* On a controller, RT uses and LT drops the item in hand (`LootButton` `'rt'`/`'lt'` in `src/game/inventory/loot-pad.ts`), and the buttons read `[RT] EAT`, `[LT] DROP` in the pad's own names.
* **The survivor reacts.** With the item over the survivor (or always, on a pad), the survivor gets a 1px rim and a line on a `/` leader saying what they think of it. `judgeItem` in `src/game/inventory/survivor-quips.ts` reads their needs and health with the sim's own refusal rules: green `need` (hungry, thirsty, bleeding, wounded, infected, badly hurt), bone `fine` (it works, nothing urgent; candy and ammo), amber `full` (the sim would refuse it) and dim `cant` (no use action). Each survivor has a voice in `src/game/inventory/survivor-voices.ts`: Night is gloomy, Bear is food-mad, Kat is a cat, Rex is a loud dino kid, Rich prices everything. The line is picked by the item's id, so it holds steady while the item is held.
* **Every move as it happens.** Each lift-and-set-down, double click, use and drop in either window is one item move (`shared/item-move.ts`), proposed to the client's move ledger (`src/game/inventory/move-ledger.ts`, glue in `use-move-ledger.ts`) and sent at once (`GameController.sendMove`); the host answers `move_ok`/`move_no` by id (`shared/item-move-sim.ts`). The grids on screen are the host's latest bag (snapshot `bagLayout` rows carry the host's ids) and open container (`viewedContainers`, kept live by `container_view` open/close) with the unanswered moves replayed on top. A refused move, or one unanswered after 2 s, flies back to its source and its target cell shakes; a container item a teammate took puffs. Closing the window sends only the container's close. Until aimed drops land, a ground drop goes down at the survivor's feet.
* **One motion source.** `src/game/inventory/move-motion.ts` holds every item motion: `fly` (from a world pickup's `item_in` event, or from the cell a double click left), `land` (set down by hand: a pop only), `back`, `puff` and `toss`. The item hops into its cell (`FLY_MS`, 420 ms), then pops in with a flash, squash and a ring (`POP_MS`, 520 ms); a puff is 300 ms of dust, a shake 240 ms. The canvas window draws it in `src/engine/loot-arrivals-draw.ts`; the page window animates a copy of the cell (`src/components/use-item-arrivals.ts`, CSS in `dual-loot-modal.css`). Neither computes its own. Reduced motion turns every flight into a landing.
* **Moves.** A double click (or the pad's quick transfer) flies the item from the cell it left to its new one (`noteMove`, in each window's own pixels). An item set down by hand, by click or drag, only lands (`notePlaced`).
* **Everything drops.** `DROPPABLE_BAG_ITEMS` is the whole bag. Bottles, spinners and fountains lie in the world as their own pickups (`bottle`, `firework`, `fountain` in `PICKUP_TYPES`, protocol 8), drawn from `src/art/fx/throwable-art.ts`, the same drawings as the HUD counters. A box of rounds goes down as an ammo pickup holding a full box of spare rounds, or what is left (`leaveAmmoOnGround` in `shared/bag-sim.ts`); rounds loaded in the gun stay there. As before, the dropper can pick it up again once they have stepped off it.
* **The log and the survivor.** Every move's drop, use or container take is written to the run log's Loot tab (`moveLines` in `src/game/inventory/bag-notes.ts`, via `GameController.sendMove`); ground pickups were already logged from their event. The survivor says a line in their own voice on a pickup or a drop (`src/game/survivor-speech.ts`, `pickup`/`drop` in `survivor-voices.ts`), drawn over them by the emote overlay. A pickup line waits 4 seconds after the last line; a drop always speaks.
* Manual uses are item moves with a `use` target.
* Wire validation ensures counts are positive integers and types match valid supplies before applying via `useSupply`.

### Playing with the inventory open
* **The game stays live on desktop.** Opening the inventory (I/B, the page modal `DualLootModal.vue` or the opt-in canvas window `canvas-loot.ts`) no longer turns the input off. The mouse aims, left click fires, right click shoves, and the play keys work: reload, weapon switch, melee, heal, throw, spinner, fountain, horn, search, walk, sprint and sneak. Panel keys (map, bag, log, pause, mute) do what they do in play, and a screen that takes over closes the grid through the existing `shouldClose` watch.
* **Choosing vs holding.** The seam is `src/game/inventory/inventory-hands.ts`. `useInventoryModal` registers the open window with `setInventoryHands` and clears it in `hide()`. Each window answers `InventoryPanel.ownsPoint(clientX, clientY)`: the page modal asks `document.elementFromPoint` whether the point is inside its root (the backdrop is `pointer-events: none`, so the gaps hit the game canvas; in the portrait/compact layout the overlay is click-through too for a mouse, `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`, and covers everything only for a finger), and the canvas window asks its own hit test and docks. *Choosing* is the pointer on that UI. *Holding* is an item in hand (`heldInfo()`), counted only while the inventory is up.
* **Clicks and the wheel.** `InputManager` drops a mouse press and a wheel step when the inventory owns the pointer (choosing or holding), so a click on a panel, grid, gear slot or button never fires and the wheel scrolls the panel. While holding, no source fires or shoves (`sample()` sends `shoot` and `melee` false); the held-item rules answer the click instead (the survivor uses, bare ground drops, the hand buttons act).
* **Keys.** The capture key handler (`inventoryKeyAction`) keeps only Esc (the window's escape, never the pause menu), I/B (close; swallowed on repeat) and R while holding (rotate the item in hand). With the hand empty R is reload. A search window passes every other key, the search key above all. Search pressed again at the container already open keeps the window (`App.vue` ignores that reopen); a window opening over an open one (a search ending under the Nearby window) first puts back what is held (`settleOpen`); every move was already sent.
* **Controller.** Beside an open inventory the pad is shared: the left stick walks and the right stick aims; LB/RB, L3 and R3 play; A/B/X/Y, Start, View and the D-pad stay the inventory's (the D-pad alone moves the loot cursor). RT and LT go to the item in hand first (use and drop) and are spent if they acted; with the hand empty they fire and shove. A container search window does not share the pad (`InventoryHands.sharesPad`, read through `inventoryPadShared()`): every button stays the game's mid-search, so A keeps counting and the D-pad still reloads.
* **Crosshair and cursor.** The crosshair is drawn over the game, hidden while the mouse is over the inventory's UI (`run-frame.ts`), and replaced by the item while holding. Over the game the crosshair is the cursor (`.is-aiming`); the page modal's panels show the OS cursor (`cursor: default` on `.dual-loot-backdrop`) and the canvas window sets the canvas cursor while over its UI. The canvas window answers a click only when its press was its own too (on its UI, or holding), so a shot swept across a grid and let go there picks nothing up. The HUD fades to 0.6 rather than 0.22 so ammo and health stay readable.
* **Touch.** Phones and tablets keep the old behaviour: opening takes the hands (`setWalkOnly`, the feet stay free) and closing gives them back.

### Searching a container
* **The container zooms out of the world.** When a container's loot window opens (a search starting, or a searched one reopened), the container's own world sprite zooms over 400 ms with an ease-out from where it stands to the stretched stage, where its grid sits over its drawing. There is no card around it: no plate, border or header box, only a small floating label (the title in amber, `SEARCHING` or `N ITEMS` dim). The survivor's side keeps its panel.
* **The sweep waits for the landing.** The sim's progress (`searchSweep`) is untouched and keeps advancing during the zoom. Both windows draw `shownSweep` instead, which holds at 0 until the zoom lands and then catches up with the sim (a whole search in 450 ms at most), never ahead of it. The `?` reveal of the loot also waits for the landing.
* **The searched prop is outlined.** While its window is open, the prop gets a 1px amber outline where it stands (`drawOutline`, the colour of the loot window's titles), so it reads as the one being searched. The outline goes when the window closes.
* **Fallbacks.** A prop off screen or under a closed roof is not drawn that frame, so the art fades in where it sits instead (180 ms). With reduced motion nothing moves: the window opens as it lands. A flipped car (`_v` look) needs nothing extra: the world draws the same art the window stretches, and only the grid data is transposed.
* **Rebuilds resume.** The page window remounts on every use or drop, and a search ending closes and reopens the same window. A reopen of the same container within 300 ms of a close rejoins the zoom where it was, so neither replays it.
* **The fly-back.** On close, a copy of the open drawing flies back to the prop for 180 ms and fades. A close that is followed straight away by a reopen of the same container never flies back.
* **Where it lives.** `src/game/inventory/container-zoom.ts` holds the shared clock and math, keyed by container id: `useInventoryModal` starts it in `showContainer` and ends it in `show()` and `hide()`, so every close path (walk-away, search end, auto-close-empty, canvas close) ends it. `EntityRenderer.drawProp` draws the outline and reports the prop's stage rect each frame, and `WorldRenderer.render` calls `noteWorldFrame`, which picks zoom, fade or still. The page window flies a clone of the stage SVG in `src/components/use-container-zoom.ts`; the canvas window lerps the rect it draws the art in (`drawStageArt` in `src/engine/loot-container-art.ts`) and skips the board until it lands. The Dev bench keeps its plate and title and never zooms.

### 4. Bunker Locker Carryover
* Banked trip receipts merge carried supplies into the bunker locker via `mergeHome(locker, gear)`.
* In the bunker, the locker screen allows transferring supplies between the player's hands and the locker.
